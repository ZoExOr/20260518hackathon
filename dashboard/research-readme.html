Multi-Agent Auto Research README

A simple explanation of the operating idea behind our RestBench restaurant agent.

Back to Dashboard
We are not asking one model to make every restaurant decision. We split the work into focused agents, let a coordinator combine their recommendations, and use a research loop to learn from each simulated day.
One sentence version The system learns how the simulation behaves, then turns that learning into safer daily actions.

Why This Exists

RestBench is an uncertain operating environment. We do not know the exact demand curve, price elasticity, supplier reliability, hidden scenario behavior, or final scoring weights. A fixed rule system can survive known cases, but it may miss hidden patterns. A full LLM agent can adapt, but it is harder to control.

The middle path is multi-agent + auto research: deterministic calculators handle risky mechanics, while a lightweight research layer watches outcomes and updates strategic assumptions.

Agent Roles

Supply Agent

Protects the restaurant from stockouts and waste.

  • Inventory and expiring batches
  • Pending orders and delivery days
  • Supplier lead time and reliability
  • Safe replenishment quantities

Demand Agent

Looks for demand and revenue opportunities.

  • Weather and weekday patterns
  • Dish sales and menu mix
  • Pricing, marketing, specials, happy hour
  • Demand surge or demand slump signals

Operations Agent

Balances staffing cost against service quality.

  • Staff level
  • Wait times and walkouts
  • Table utilization
  • Kitchen bottlenecks

Research Agent

Turns daily outcomes into reusable learning.

  • Summarizes what happened yesterday
  • Tracks hypotheses about demand and suppliers
  • Records experiment outcomes
  • Maintains long-term memory

Coordinator

Combines recommendations into one action list.

  • Resolves conflicts between agents
  • Orders actions in a stable sequence
  • Keeps the final plan coherent

Risk Agent

Checks the plan before it reaches the API.

  • No duplicate orders
  • No cash reserve breach
  • No invalid menu or price action
  • No obvious strategic conflict

Daily Flow

1. ObserveRead the current restaurant state.
2. AnalyzeSupply, demand, and operations produce recommendations.
3. ResearchMemory adds hypotheses and reminders.
4. DecideCoordinator builds the final actions.
5. LearnDay result updates memory for tomorrow.

What Auto Research Means Here

Auto research does not mean reading academic papers. It means running a closed loop over the simulation itself:

  1. Collect evidence from observations and day results.
  2. Form hypotheses about demand, pricing, suppliers, and staffing.
  3. Test those hypotheses through low-risk actions.
  4. Update the strategy based on what happened.

Practical V1

For the hackathon, the first usable version should stay simple. Start with deterministic calculators for inventory, expiry, pending orders, delivery timing, and cash safety. Add a lightweight research note each day. Let the coordinator merge recommendations. Keep the risk layer deterministic.

This gives us a system that is easier to debug than a pure LLM agent and more adaptable than static rules.
